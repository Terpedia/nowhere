# Manuscript outline

## Provisional title

**Is absinthe psychedelic? A Terpedia evidence map of psychedelic biochemistry, absinthe chemistry, and competing mechanisms**

## Central question

Do absinthe's known constituents and preparation-level effects meet a defensible biochemical and phenomenological definition of a psychedelic, or are historical claims better explained by ethanol, toxicology, non-5-HT2A pharmacology, and context?

## Planned sections

1. **Introduction** — historical claim, modern meaning of “psychedelic,” and why beverage-level attribution is difficult.
2. **Definitions and taxonomy** — classic serotonergic psychedelics versus dissociatives, deliriants, cannabinoids, sedatives, convulsants, and broadly psychoactive substances.
3. **Biochemistry of psychedelics** — 5-HT2A agonism/activation; downstream signaling; cortical excitation; network-level and plasticity hypotheses; pharmacokinetics; dose and set/setting. Keep receptor mechanism, subjective phenomenology, and therapeutic outcome separate.
4. **Terpedia methods** — source selection, entity resolution, stereochemical handling, COA provenance, literature extraction, and evidence-level coding.
5. **Absinthe chemical inventory** — botanical stages, volatile versus nonvolatile fractions, reported GC-MS observations, and uncertainty in attribution.
6. **Candidate mechanism review** — thujones, fenchone/pinocamphone, anetholes, linalool, and ethanol; compare known/claimed targets with 5-HT2A criteria.
7. **Exposure plausibility** — serving-level dose, matrix effects, metabolism, CNS availability, and the distinction between neat compounds and finished beverage.
8. **Historical and experimental evidence** — absinthism narratives, controlled alcohol comparisons, and what would count as a preparation-level psychedelic study.
9. **Synthesis** — evidence matrix and classification: observed, directly characterized, mechanistically supported, structurally possible, or unestablished.
10. **Limitations and next experiments** — independent chemical analysis, receptor panel, PK, and randomized ethanol-matched human study.

## Primary hypotheses

- **H1 classic psychedelic:** absinthe produces a psychedelic state through 5-HT2A agonism from one or more constituents.
- **H2 non-psychedelic psychoactivity:** reported effects are explained mainly by ethanol and non-5-HT2A constituents, including GABAergic/toxicological activity.
- **H3 context/mixture:** preparation, dose, expectation, and weak multi-target effects explain variability without satisfying the classic psychedelic definition.

## Minimum decisive experiment

Use a chemically characterized absinthe preparation and an ethanol-matched control, quantify thujones and major volatiles, measure plasma exposure, and preregister a blinded battery containing validated subjective psychedelic measures plus physiological and cognitive outcomes. A positive result would still require evidence that effects track 5-HT2A activation rather than ethanol or toxicity.
