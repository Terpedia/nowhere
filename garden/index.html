Nowhere's End / Absinthe Garden

What can grow in Monument?

Zone 5b / high elevation / short season

The practical answer is selective. Hardy wormwoods, hyssop, mint, yarrow, calendula, cornflower, and several shrubs are realistic local crops. Warm- season seed crops are possible but less consistent, while tropical, citrus, and Mediterranean materials belong in a greenhouse or the supply chain.

The growing brief

Build around the microclimate

Monument sits at roughly 7,000–7,400 feet. For planning, use a late-May last frost, a late-September first frost, and approximately 125–140 frost-free days. South-facing walls, raised beds, wind protection, and drip irrigation will make a meaningful difference.

“Feasible” below means the plant can produce useful material, not merely survive one winter. Seed, root, flower, and essential-oil yields can be much less predictable than survival—especially for anise, fennel, gentian, and genepi.

Best local candidates

Start here

  • Grand and Roman wormwood
  • Hyssop, lemon balm, mint, and yarrow
  • Calendula and cornflower
  • Coriander and hardy roses
  • Elderflower and juniper as long-term shrubs

Production note

Garden for identity, source for consistency

Local cultivation is especially valuable for the signature botanicals: wormwood, hyssop, lemon balm, yarrow, and flowers. For anise, fennel, citrus, roots, and exotic spices, purchased lots or contracted supply will produce more repeatable chemistry and sensory results.
