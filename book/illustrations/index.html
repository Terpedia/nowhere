The Illustration Bible

Plate · diagram · relic · apparition

The visual language for a fully illustrated Book of Potions: botanical exactness, Symbolist atmosphere, laboratory clarity, and the tactile authority of an old reference volume.

Every image must do one of three things: show what the potion is, reveal what it does, or enlarge what it means. The square page is treated as a room: a plate can breathe, and a caption can remain part of the composition.

The visual thesis · 12 × 12 edition

Beauty is a form of evidence.

The image must seduce the eye before the caption teaches it how to look.

Beauty does not mean decorative vagueness. The botanical plate identifies the plant. The mechanism diagram earns its lines. The apparition is clearly an apparition. The archive image carries a source. The image may open the door, but the caption tells us which room we have entered. The square format gives each plate room to behave as both image and page.

Now complete: the wormwood whole-plant plate, the Monument garden plate, and the louche mechanism plate have finished large-format assets, alt text, and caption boundaries in the manuscript.

Four plate families

One book, four visual instruments

I

The Botanical Plate

Pressed-looking specimen, whole plant, root, seed, flower, leaf, and labeled plant parts. Exact enough for identification; beautiful enough to feel talismanic.

II

The Mechanism Diagram

Glass, oil, water, molecule, receptor, still, color, and louche shown as clean explanatory systems over parchment or deep green.

III

The Historical Relic

Bottle, spoon, fountain, café, label, advertisement, manuscript, map, law, or archival fragment with provenance in the caption.

IV

The Visionary Plate

Green Goddess, falling star, dragon, garden, sound, and ritual. Explicitly interpretive; never disguised as historical evidence.

Palette

The Nowhere spectrum

  • Verdigris green: the living plant and the traditional verte.
  • Deep forest: shadow, history, and the unlit laboratory.
  • Parchment: source, memory, and the botanical page.
  • Oxidized gold: ritual hardware, labels, and illumination.
  • Cobalt: Bleue, distance, air, and clear spirits.
  • Vermilion: Crimson, warning, blood, and floral heat.
  • Louche yellow: transformation, instability, and the Detour turn.

Materials

What the pages should feel like

  • Rag paper, deckled edges, and faint fiber
  • Engraved linework and imperfect ink registration
  • Translucent vellum overlays for molecules and louche stages
  • Blackened copper, silver, glass, wood, and stone
  • Marginal symbols that function as a visual index
  • Occasional full-bleed color after long monochrome sequences

Caption protocol

Every image tells the truth about itself

  • Name the subject, date, maker, or plant when known.
  • Link archival material to its source or collection.
  • Mark commissioned art as interpretation.
  • Separate reconstruction from surviving evidence.
  • Never use visionary imagery to imply pharmacological proof.
  • Keep alt text descriptive, specific, and free of marketing claims.
