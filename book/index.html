Nowhere's End / Working Manuscript

A Book of Potions

History · Mythology · Pharmacology

Nowhere's End Absinthe will become a large-format, fully illustrated book about what happens when a bitter medicinal plant becomes a drink, then a myth, then a chemical system, then a personal ritual.

This is the working architecture: part illuminated grimoire, part botanical atlas, part laboratory notebook, and part visual history of the Green Fairy.

The governing question

What is a potion?

A potion is not only what it contains. It is what the body senses, what the mind expects, what history has taught us to see, and what the ritual makes possible.

The book will hold those readings together without confusing them. A molecule can be described precisely; a myth can be honored as myth; a subjective experience can remain meaningful without becoming a medical claim. The result should feel mysterious because the subject is deep, not because the evidence is hidden.

The object

A book built to be handled

Format: large-format, landscape-leaning coffee-table book with generous margins, translucent overlays, tipped-in plates, and a visual rhythm that rewards browsing as much as sequential reading.

Audience: curious drinkers, design-minded readers, botanical enthusiasts, historians of intoxication, and anyone drawn to the border between evidence and myth.

The promise

Beautiful enough to leave open

Every spread must offer an immediate image, one memorable idea, and one reason to turn the page. Dense research belongs in the architecture, captions, marginalia, and appendices—not in walls of undifferentiated prose.

The book should feel like an artifact before it is read, and become more trustworthy the longer it is read.

Editorial rule

Known, believed, experienced

Every spread should make its mode legible. Historical claims need sources. Pharmacology needs calibrated language. Symbolism can be expansive. The psychedelic interpretation remains available as a cultural and subjective reading without pretending that absinthe is a confirmed classic psychedelic.

Research protocol

Evidence with a visible spine

  • Primary sources for historical quotations and archival claims
  • Museum, library, pharmacology, and regulatory sources for factual claims
  • Separate observed batch data from published research
  • Label traditional medicine as tradition, not current treatment advice
  • Use “may,” “is consistent with,” and “has not been demonstrated” where evidence requires it
  • Keep a source ledger tied to chapter, spread, image, and caption

A+ completion standard

What finished means

  • Every major claim is sourced or explicitly marked as interpretation
  • Every potion has a formula, image, literary motif, and listening brief
  • Every major botanical has a plate, molecule, sensory note, and safety note
  • The louche and color stories are experimentally documented
  • The visual system remains coherent across history, science, and myth
  • The book can be browsed as art and read as a serious reference
