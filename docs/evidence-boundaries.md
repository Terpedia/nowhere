# Evidence boundaries

| Level | Permitted claim |
|---|---|
| Observed in Terpedia artifact | The repository reports a compound, concentration, or source attribution. |
| Directly characterized | A primary experiment measures a target, exposure, behavior, or subjective effect for the exact molecule/preparation. |
| Mechanistically supported | Multiple compatible biochemical observations support a mechanism, with dose and exposure context. |
| Unestablished | The claim remains plausible, indirect, contradictory, or unsupported. |

## Rules

- Do not call a molecule psychedelic because it is psychoactive, sedating, convulsant, anxiolytic, bitter, or biologically active.
- Do not substitute GABA-A antagonism, GABA-A modulation, 5-HT3 antagonism, or general CNS stimulation for 5-HT2A agonism.
- Preserve stereochemistry and chemical identity: alpha- and beta-thujone are separate records; cis- and trans-anethole are separate records.
- Separate the volatile fraction from nonvolatile botanical constituents and from ethanol.
- A GC-MS library match supports chemical identification in the analyzed sample; it does not establish receptor activity, bioavailability, or subjective effects.
- Historical accounts of “absinthism” are not equivalent to controlled evidence of psychedelic experience.

## Proposed operational definition

For the primary analysis, “classic serotonergic psychedelic” means a compound or preparation with evidence of centrally relevant 5-HT2A receptor agonism/activation and a reproducible alteration of perception, cognition, or self-experience at plausible human exposure. Other hallucinogens can be described, but should not be merged with the classic serotonergic category.
